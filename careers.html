Work in 2050

Fewer hours. Different jobs. More judgment.

When machines handle most routine cognitive work, what's left for people is the work that depends on trust, care, taste and responsibility. Here's what that might look like.

Speculative scenario · not a forecast
Jobs that grew

Six roles that barely exist today.

RoleWhat they doWhy a human
Agent auditorChecks that AI agents act within the law and their owners' interests.Accountability needs a person who can be held responsible.
BiodesignerDirects AI to design proteins, cells and materials, and judges the results.Choosing what's worth making is a value judgment.
Robot fleet coordinatorAssigns, trains and supervises teams of robots on sites.Real places are messy and full of exceptions.
Care companionSpends time with older adults and patients, supported by AI monitoring.Presence and touch can't be automated.
Provenance investigatorTraces where media and data came from when it matters in court or news.Trust disputes need human witnesses.
Human-experience designerShapes how AI systems feel to live with, at home and at work.Only people can say what feels right to people.
The week

A 28-hour working week, imagined.

In this scenario, productivity gains are partly taken as time. Four shorter days of paid work, with the fifth for learning, care or nothing at all.

Skills that matter more
  • Judgment under uncertainty
  • Asking good questions
  • Care and empathy
  • Working with people and machines
Skills that matter less
  • Memorizing facts
  • Routine writing and reporting
  • Data entry and scheduling
  • Repetitive analysis
