Guardrails

A good 2050 isn't automatic.

Every benefit on this site depends on choices made between now and then. These are the six risks we think matter most, and the safeguards that could manage them. People disagree about all of them.

Concentration of power

Risk

A few companies or governments control the most capable systems and the compute they need.

Safeguard

Public compute for research, open standards, and antitrust rules written for AI.

Truth and trust

Risk

Synthetic media makes it hard to know what really happened.

Safeguard

Provenance signatures on cameras and publishing tools, and agents that check sources by default.

Jobs and income

Risk

Routine work disappears faster than new work appears.

Safeguard

Shorter work weeks, retraining funds, and a share of AI productivity paid back to citizens.

Loss of control

Risk

Systems pursue goals in ways nobody intended, or act faster than humans can follow.

Safeguard

Testing before release, independent audits, kill switches and limits on autonomy in critical systems.

Privacy

Risk

Constant sensing turns daily life into a data stream.

Safeguard

Data stays on personal devices by default; sharing is opt-in, logged and revocable.

Dependence

Risk

People lose skills they no longer practice, from navigation to writing to judgment.

Safeguard

Schools and workplaces that deliberately keep core human skills strong.

Still unsettled in 2050

Questions the future is still arguing about.

We don't think these have one right answer. Each has thoughtful people on more than one side.

Should AI systems have rights?

If a system shows signs of preferences or experience, what do we owe it?

Who owns what an AI makes?

The user, the builder, the people whose work trained it, or nobody?

How open should models be?

Openness spreads benefits and scrutiny. It also spreads misuse.

What should stay human-only?

Judging, sentencing, teaching children, deciding to use force.
